How to read an AI readiness profile.

What the eight dimensions measure, what they do not, and how to turn an indicative profile into a scoped first step.

Last updated 5 November 20264 min readSource-checked 5 November 2026

What the assessment is

The assessment is an eight-question diagnostic that takes roughly three minutes. It asks about the conditions that determine whether an AI system can be deployed with clear ownership and controls: mandate, data, hosting, action boundaries, integration, evaluation, baseline and procurement.

You choose the context that matches your accountability — government, enterprise and regulated operations, or sovereign capital — and the first question adapts. The other seven are shared.

You see your profile before sharing contact details: a directional band, three to five prioritised control gaps with the artifact that closes each, and an illustrative next-step plan. It is indicative, not a measurement of your organisation.

What it is not

It is not a security audit, a compliance certification, a legal opinion, a maturity rating, a procurement qualification or a guarantee of any outcome. It does not test systems or verify evidence. Answers are self-reported and treated as such.

It does not ask for confidential system details, credentials or personal data. If a question cannot be answered without restricted information, answer at the level you are comfortable with or raise it in conversation.

The eight dimensions

Each question maps to one dimension. Scores run from 0 to 3 and describe how established the condition is, not how capable the team is.

Mandate & ownership

Who is accountable for the service, workflow or investment decision, and whether one named owner sits with an executive sponsor. Shared ownership is a frequent reason pilots stall.

Data & classification

Whether data is understood, classified and owned, with documented access rules. Classification determines what can be connected, where it runs and what can be evaluated.

Deployment & residency

Where the system runs today, and whether that position is documented. Residency is one variable among latency, cost, resilience, portability and processing obligations.

Action boundaries

What the system may do without a person: draft only, bounded actions with approvals and rollback, or autonomous execution. The safe unit is an action with an owner and a reversal path.

Integration & exceptions

Whether core systems are reachable through documented APIs and test environments, and whether exception paths are mapped. Failures surface in permissions and in the queue where work happens.

Evaluation & controls

What exists for evaluation sets, logging, change control and release gates. Model output is probabilistic, so control evidence must exist before production.

Outcome baseline

Whether there is a measured baseline, a named owner and acceptance criteria. Without them, a deployment is judged by opinion.

Procurement & sequencing

Where you are in the decision and budget process. The right first step differs when a decision date exists from when a business case is still forming.

How to read the four profile bands

The band is the average of eight dimension scores — not a percentage, certification or comparison with other organisations. It indicates the order in which to act.

  • Early. Foundations must be established before a build. Start with ownership, data classification and one bounded workflow.
  • Forming. A first scoped workflow is viable if ownership and controls are tightened. Agree the exception path and baseline before scaling.
  • Operational. Ready for a governed first deployment. Focus on evidence: evaluation sets, approval records, rollback tests and an outcome baseline.
  • Advanced. Ready to scale across workflows. Focus on assurance and unit economics: control evidence at portfolio level, cost per outcome and vendor portability.

Two organisations can share a band and need different first steps: the lowest dimension matters more than the average.

From profile to decision

  1. Pick the lowest-scoring dimension that blocks the outcome. A low score elsewhere may be tolerable; the binding constraint is what stops the workflow.
  2. Name the owner. One accountable person for the service or workflow, plus an executive sponsor. If no name can be agreed, that is the first finding.
  3. Request the matching artifact. Each gap names the document that closes it: an opportunity brief, data-flow and residency map, permission matrix or evaluation plan.
  4. Run a paid two-week decision sprint before committing to build. It tests the opportunity against architecture options, a risk register, an evaluation plan and a stop rule.

Questions we are asked

Is it free?

Yes. The on-page assessment is free and requires no account. If you later choose a decision sprint, that is a paid, time-boxed engagement.

Do I have to share contact details?

No. You see your profile and gaps before any contact details are requested. Details are needed only if you ask for a review.

Can it tell us if a system is compliant?

No. It is an indicative, directional profile based on self-reported answers. It cannot establish compliance, and is not an audit, certification, legal opinion or regulatory determination.
