From national AI mandates to governed public services.

What the UAE's agentic-AI mandate changes for service owners, and how DLI turns one service journey into a governed deployment.

Last updated 5 November 20265 min readSource-checked 5 November 2026

What actually changed

On 23 April 2026 the UAE Cabinet announced a national objective: transition 50% of government services and operations to agentic AI within two years. It set a direction and a deadline, not a plan for any single entity.

The implementation detail arrived on 18 May 2026, when the Cabinet, chaired by Mohammed bin Rashid, approved a federal framework for the Agentic AI Project. That approval covered a first package of services across four categories — Citizens' Services, Residents' Services, Business Sector Services and General Public Services — together with a programme to train 80,000 federal employees across five occupational categories.

The framework also assigns implementation teams inside each entity, headed by the respective Minister or Head of entity, and requires targets and assessment indicators. The two dates describe a national implementation programme with named owners and measurable obligations — not a single procurement, and not something one vendor or platform will deliver.

For a service owner, the question is narrower: which journey, whose accountability, what evidence, and what happens when the agent is wrong?

The gap between a mandate and a working service

National ambition does not remove service-level constraints. Five constraints decide whether a pilot survives real cases.

Service redesign. A mandate counts services; it does not redesign them. Agentic assistance changes eligibility checks, document requirements and handoffs between teams. If the underlying process is unchanged, an agent only helps a slow queue arrive faster at the same manual decision.

Identity and consent. An agent acting for a resident needs delegated authority, a defined scope and a record of what was consented to. Whatever identity layer the entity already uses has to carry that delegation, and the agent has to stop when authority is absent, narrow or expired.

Exceptions and appeals. Agents meet edge cases immediately: incomplete documents, contested facts, bereavement, disability, a change in business status. Each needs a route to a named human decision-maker, an explanation the applicant can understand and a documented appeal path. Designing these after launch is a common cause of reversal.

Arabic and English quality. Arabic is not a translation layer bolted onto an English flow. Formal and everyday registers, mixed-language case notes and entity-specific terminology all affect extraction and drafting quality. Evaluation has to run in both languages, on the case types the entity actually receives.

Procurement and assurance evidence. New programmes attract scrutiny from audit, legal and information security. Teams need evaluation results, data-flow maps, decision records and rollback evidence before those reviews, not after. The framework's targets and assessment indicators make that evidence part of delivery, not an optional extra.

A governed service pattern

The pattern we use starts small and stays auditable.

  1. Choose one service journey and name the owner. Pick a journey with real volume, a measurable outcome and a single accountable executive. Write the mandate down: what the agent may do, for whom, and what success means. A second journey waits.
  2. Baseline the journey on 90 days of history. Reconstruct volumes, cycle times, rejection reasons, handoffs and exception rates from existing case data. Without a baseline, no later improvement claim is defensible.
  3. Map identity, consent, exception and appeal paths. Draw the flow before the model. Mark where authority is asserted, where data crosses a boundary, where a human must decide, and where a decision can be contested.
  4. Bound agent actions and approvals. Separate what the agent may draft, what it may execute and what requires approval. Set spend, scope and data limits, and log every action with the evidence used.
  5. Evaluate in Arabic and English on real case types. Build a test set from de-identified historical cases across both languages, including the awkward ones. Score accuracy, abstention, escalation correctness and explanation quality, then agree thresholds in advance.
  6. Operate with monthly outcome reporting. Report cycle time, completion rate, exception rate, appeal outcomes and incidents to the owner monthly. Review scope quarterly and expand only on evidence.

What DLI delivers

DLI is an independent architecture and engineering firm. We work in three shapes for public-sector teams: an advisory and architecture decision sprint, forward-deployed engineering for one workflow, and managed operations once a service is live. Each is designed for one service at a time, with the entity's own owners in the decision seat.
